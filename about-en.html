Notepad — a browser-based text editor for Arabic and English

Notepad is a free, open-source tool for editing Arabic and English text right in your browser: no sign-up, no server, no ads. Its first version was built after reverse-engineering Windows Notepad (Notepad.exe) with REA and Ghidra.

Why Notepad?

Most online notepads are plain text boxes that ignore encodings, right-to-left languages, and offline use. This one keeps your files in their original encoding and line endings, supports Arabic and RTL, and installs like a real app on phone and desktop.

Key features

  • Open and save TXT, LOG, MD and similar files with an explicit encoding.
  • UTF-8, UTF-8 with BOM, UTF-16 LE/BE, and Arabic/Latin ANSI (Windows-1256 / Windows-1252).
  • Automatic encoding and line-ending (CRLF/LF/CR) detection on open.
  • Fast find & replace with regular expressions and match-case.
  • Rename the document, local autosave, and recovery of unsaved documents.
  • Live stats: line, column, characters, and lines.
  • ‎.LOG‎ timestamp feature and one-click date/time insertion.
  • Dark mode, font settings, word wrap, printing, and an Arabic/English interface.
  • Installable PWA that works offline after the first visit.

Who is it for?

  • Anyone who wants to write quick Arabic or English notes on any device.
  • Developers editing config, log, or small source files.
  • People receiving UTF-16/ANSI text files who need to read or fix them without office suites.
  • Users who prefer lightweight tools that work offline and collect no data.

Is my data safe?

Yes. The app never uploads your content: unsaved docs are stored in your browser's IndexedDB, settings in localStorage, and files are written to your device through the browser's File System Access API. No account, no analytics of your text, no ads.

Install on your phone

  • Android (Chrome/Edge): browser menu → “Install app” or “Add to Home screen”.
  • iPhone/iPad (Safari): Share button → “Add to Home Screen”.
  • Windows/Mac (Chrome/Edge): the install icon in the address bar or the browser menu.

FAQ

Is Notepad really free?
Yes — free and open source under the MIT license, with no ads or subscriptions.
Does it need internet?
No, after the first visit it works offline thanks to a Service Worker.
Can it open large files?
Yes, within browser memory limits; very large files (tens of MB) may need splitting.
Does it save automatically?
Yes, the current document is autosaved locally and a restore bar appears on reload.
Why can't I save directly in some browsers?
The File System Access API is available in Chrome and Edge; other browsers download the file instead.
